Afrobeats track-classification codebook
Draft for capstone use — evidence standard remains provisional

Purpose
Classify tracks for the capstone question: Which international markets offer
the greatest growth opportunities for emerging Afrobeats artists?

1. Study definition

For this study, Afrobeats is a track-level genre classification: the track is
labeled Afrobeats when reliable, track-specific evidence identifies it as
Afrobeats or clearly places it within the contemporary Afrobeats genre used in
this study.

Do not infer the genre from the artist's nationality, name, language,
collaborators, or the market where the track charted. Artist or market context
may guide research but cannot establish a track's classification.

Needs external source support: A cited definition of contemporary Afrobeats
and the authority or sources accepted as evidence for genre attribution.

2. Afrobeats versus Afrobeat

Treat Afrobeats and Afrobeat as separate labels:
- Afrobeats refers to the contemporary genre intended by this study.
- Afrobeat refers to a distinct genre label. A track identified only as
  Afrobeat is not Afrobeats for this codebook.
- If reliable sources use both labels, or use a spelling without enough
  context to tell which genre is intended, label the track
  uncertain/unclassified until resolved.

Needs external source support: A sourced distinction between the genres,
including how to interpret source labels that may use the terms inconsistently.

3. Collaborations and cross-genre tracks

Classify the track, not every artist on it.
- A collaboration qualifies when reliable evidence identifies the track itself
  as Afrobeats, even if one or more credited artists are associated with other
  genres.
- A track described as a fusion or cross-genre work qualifies when the
  evidence explicitly includes Afrobeats as a genre or substantial stylistic
  component.
- An Afrobeats-associated artist's feature or appearance alone is not
  sufficient.
- If sources identify only another genre, classify as not Afrobeats. If
  evidence is mixed or too vague to decide, use uncertain/unclassified.

Needs external source support: The threshold for treating Afrobeats as a
substantial stylistic component in a fusion track. Until supported, prefer
explicit track-level genre descriptions and send borderline cases to
uncertain/unclassified.

4. Classification labels

Afrobeats
  Adequate track-specific evidence supports the study definition.

Not Afrobeats
  Adequate evidence identifies the track as another genre, or as Afrobeat
  alone, with no qualifying Afrobeats evidence.

Uncertain/unclassified
  Evidence is missing, ambiguous, conflicting, or insufficient to apply the
  rules consistently. Do not force a binary decision when evidence is
  inconclusive.

5. Evidence to record for each track

Record enough information for another researcher to reproduce the decision:
- Track title and stable identifier, such as the dataset's track_id.
- Recording/release version when sources distinguish versions.
- Classification label and date of decision.
- Source name, source type, direct URL or persistent identifier, access date,
  and relevant quotation or concise paraphrase.
- Whether the evidence is specifically about the track, an album, or only the
  artist.
- The source's exact genre wording, preserving distinctions such as
  Afrobeats and Afrobeat.
- Decision rationale, including collaboration or cross-genre considerations.
- Reviewer name/initials and any adjudication outcome.

Needs external source support: An approved source hierarchy and minimum
evidence threshold. Until agreed, record all sources consulted and avoid
treating a single weak or unspecific mention as conclusive.

6. Conflicts and ambiguous cases

When sources conflict, record each source and its wording. Prefer direct,
track-specific evidence over artist biographies or broad catalog descriptions,
subject to the source hierarchy adopted for the study. If credible
track-specific sources remain in conflict, label uncertain/unclassified and
flag for review.

For ambiguous wording—especially Afrobeat versus Afrobeats—do not silently
normalize the label. Seek clarifying evidence; otherwise leave the track
uncertain/unclassified. Keep a decision log so that later adjudications are
applied consistently.

Needs external source support: The final source hierarchy, conflict-resolution
rule, and who adjudicates unresolved cases.

7. Prohibited shortcuts

A track must not be classified as Afrobeats solely because:
- An artist is Nigerian, Ghanaian, or from another country associated with the
  genre.
- An artist's name or public image seems culturally associated with Afrobeats.
- The track charted in an African or other particular market.
- The artist is commonly described as an Afrobeats artist.
- A collaborator is associated with Afrobeats.

These facts may help locate sources, but classification requires track-specific
evidence.

Evidence standard — provisional

This standard is provisional and has not been externally validated.
- Track-specific evidence is required.
- Record every source consulted and its exact genre wording.
- Prefer direct track-level sources over artist-level descriptions.
- If evidence is missing, ambiguous, or conflicting, use
  uncertain/unclassified.
- Do not classify a track as not Afrobeats merely because no Afrobeats evidence
  was found.
